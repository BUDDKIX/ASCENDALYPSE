ASCENDALYPSE — Project description

Genre: 2D precision platformer, inspired by Jump King
Jam theme: BOUNCE BACK

Two hundred years after humanity vanished, lightning strikes the antenna on Floor 10. The surge follows the wiring down to a generator on Floor 0. Beside it, a moss-covered service robot wakes up, still tethered to the machine. His creator is slumped at the workbench. When the bones begin to move, the Unit has to reckon with the one instruction his creator left him: “Never harm a human.”

The jam theme is BOUNCE BACK. The Unit comes back to life after two centuries without power. His spring jump turns that idea into something you can feel: hold a charge on a narrow landing, aim, then let go. Miss the landing over water and you can return for the part you lost. The harder recovery is the Unit’s own: how can a machine built to protect people fight the remains of the person who built him?

Characters
- The Unit was built for domestic and maintenance work. Rust and moss cover his casing. During the Floor 0 encounter, dialogue stays in a fixed bottom-of-screen panel with his face portrait on the left and text to the right; Space advances one line at a time.
- The Creator, called the Maker in the demo, is the engineer whose skeleton rests on Floor 0. Parasitic moss animates his remains. The encounter puts the Unit’s directive in conflict with his survival.
- The Overgrown are the planned enemies of the upper tower. Fungi and roots bind bones to discarded machinery; some creatures will guard parts of the lift.

Playable now: The Spark (prologue) and The Directive & The Mutation (Floor 0)
Two centuries after humanity vanished, lightning reboots a maintenance robot inside an overgrown robotics facility. Escape begins at the generator and ends at the lift.

Play opens the factory cinematic. Tap Space eight times, or hold both controller triggers, to free the tether. After the explosion, the ASCENDALYPSE title card appears above the space-to-play prompt; a fresh Space press starts play. The robot walks and steers with the arrows or left stick; hold and release Space or controller X to spring-jump, with the supplied jump cue playing on takeoff. R or controller L2 cancels a charge; E returns to the active checkpoint.

F scans the Maker’s remains. Space advances dialogue one line at a time: “Master...?”, the four-line internal conflict on the monster’s first hit, “I must... BOUNCE BACK!” on its next hit, and “Farewell... master.” after its defeat animation. Combat pauses during the hit dialogues; resuming grants a one-second damage-invulnerability window. The remains transform into the Nature Monster, represented for now by the existing Maker animation frames. Keyboard X or controller R2 performs a short crescent attack at any time during active gameplay, with a half-second recovery; attacks damage the monster only during its fight. It takes eight hits and delays its lunge late in the fight. Two enemy hits cost one part. Dropped parts become collectible after five seconds. Each enemy hit or lost part plays the robot damage cue and rumbles a connected controller; each landed monster hit also rumbles. At five lost parts, a separate Game Over screen plays its cue and displays “GAME OVER” with a Space/controller-X respawn prompt; Q remains an alternate restart key. Jump, attack, enemy-death, robot-damage and game-over sounds use the supplied SFX. Scan, monster-vocal and mutation-impact SFX players remain empty Inspector hooks.

Defeating the Nature Monster opens the Floodway gate. The route has 32 jumps above the water. Falling costs a part; the robot respawns at its checkpoint while the part stays on the last safe landing. Touching the cyan beacon on landing 18 updates the checkpoint; a small white CHECKPOINT label floats above its light. F powers the lift and displays “move to the next level”. Floor 0 ends there.

Planned, not playable yet
Act II · The Ascent (Floors 1–9). The plan is to climb the broken lift shaft floor by floor, recovering lift components guarded by the Overgrown. These are separate from the Unit’s own recoverable parts on Floor 0. Higher platforms give way to exposed steel and rain; a missed jump can cost part of the climb.

Act III · The Guardian & the Sky (Floors 9–10). An apex Overgrown guards the last lift mechanism on Floor 9. Beyond it lies the open terrace. The planned ending has no further fight: the storm clears, and the Unit steps outside under his own power.

Art, audio and haptic direction
Floor 0 uses wet concrete, rust and deep moss greens so the Unit’s eyes, lightning and checkpoint light stand out. For the ascent, the proposed art plan groups the tower into three zones: flooded basement and lobby (0–3), overgrown offices (4–7), and storm-battered penthouse and terrace (8–10). More daylight would reach each successive zone. Sound and haptic targets include a rising generator hum, trigger rumble and a cut to silence at detonation, with small mechanical whirs and synth chirps in place of voiced dialogue. These are production targets, not promised features of this build.

Music integration: the reference score starts with Play and continues through the explosion and blank transition. At the title cut, the separate `opening/title_card.tscn` scene starts `audio/gameplay_music.wav` and displays the title and Space prompt together. Space hides the title layer and unlocks gameplay while the track continues looping.
